A clean break rarely needs much ceremony—especially when the decision is made unilaterally.

Revoking permissions, cutting off contact, redrawing boundaries: these actions speak clearly enough. When the people of the past are no longer needed, there is naturally no reason to preserve the relationships of the past either. No notice, no discussion, no handover—not even the willingness to offer a formal ending—is itself an unambiguous statement.

Adults ought to respect decisions, especially the ones already made on their behalf without their participation. When someone is eager to draw a hard line, there is no reason to leave them any gray area. Past goodwill does not create a permanent obligation, nor does past contribution mean signing up for an indefinite renewal.

Of course, re-establishing order is a manager's prerogative, and reassigning responsibilities is a manager's freedom. Yet authority and accountability have always come as a pair: whoever chooses to reclaim the former should be capable of carrying the latter. One cannot insist on strict boundaries when setting rules, only to remember old hands when someone is needed to catch the fallout.

The systems may still keep running, and the services may remain online. Only now, their operation belongs to a new chapter of management.
